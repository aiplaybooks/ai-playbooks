AAI Playbooks

Invest your first $100 the smart way. 7 prompts that teach you what most beginners never learn:

Thanks for following! Everything from the post, ready to copy. Fill in the [BRACKETS] with your details.

01

The Plain-English Tutor

Finally understand the words

Explain these investing terms like I'm a smart 15-year-old, with one everyday example each: index fund, ETF, stock, bond, dividend, expense ratio, diversification, compound interest. Then quiz me with 5 QUESTIONS.

TIP Answer the quiz out loud before you move on — that's how the terms actually stick.

02

The Goal Sorter

Which money goes where

Help me sort my goals before investing: [LIST GOALS WITH AMOUNTS AND DATES]. For each, tell me if it's short, medium or long term, why that matters for risk, and which goals should stay in SAVINGS instead of investments.

TIP Money you need within 2-3 years usually shouldn't be invested at all.

03

The Risk Profiler

How much drop can you handle

Ask me 8 questions, one at a time, to understand my risk tolerance: income stability, emergency fund, timeline, how I'd react to a 30% drop. Then describe my profile and what it usually means for a portfolio, WITHOUT recommending specific products.

TIP Answer honestly about the 30% drop question — that's the one people fake.

04

The Compound Visualizer

See what time does to money

Show me a table of what [$MONTHLY AMOUNT] a month becomes after 5, 10, 20 and 30 years at 4%, 6% and 8% yearly returns. Then show the same if I start 5 years later, and explain the difference in TWO SENTENCES.

05

The Fee Detective

Small fees, big difference

Compare the long-term cost of a 0.1%, 0.5% and 1.5% yearly fee on [$AMOUNT] invested for [YEARS] years with a 6% return. Show the money lost to fees in a table and list 5 QUESTIONS to ask about fees before I open any account.

TIP A 1.5% fee can quietly eat tens of thousands of dollars over 30 years.

06

The Myth Buster

What beginners get wrong

List the 10 most common beginner investing mistakes (timing the market, chasing hype, no emergency fund ...). For each: why people do it, what it can cost, and one SIMPLE RULE that prevents it.

TIP Keep this list open the next time a hot tip shows up in your feed.

07

The Advisor Prep

Walk into the bank prepared

I'm meeting a financial advisor about investing [$AMOUNT] for [GOAL]. Write 12 questions to ask about fees, conflicts of interest, risk, and how they get paid, plus the RED FLAGS in their answers that should make me walk away.

TIP "How do you get paid on my account" is the one question that reveals the most.
