Thanks for following! Everything from the post, ready to copy. Fill in the [BRACKETS] with your details.

01

The Niche Generator

Turn your own life into 10 rated ideas

Act as a niche research expert. My interests: [INTERESTS]. My skills: [SKILLS]. People I know well: [AUDIENCES]. Combine them into 10 specific niches (a person + a problem), and rate each on pain, ability to pay, and how REACHABLE they are for me.

TIP Be brutally specific about the audience — "new dog owners in apartments" beats "pet lovers" every time.

02

The Demand Detective

Proof that people actually want it

For the niche [NICHE], tell me where to look for proof of demand: which forums, search terms, marketplaces and review sites, what signals to look for (complaints, paid products, 'how do I' questions), and what would count as NO DEMAND.

TIP If you can't find people already paying for a rough version of the fix, that's the real signal to notice.

03

The Competitor X-Ray

Find the gap everyone else leaves open

Here are 3-5 competitors in [NICHE]: [NAMES, LINKS OR DESCRIPTIONS]. Compare their offer, price, audience and weak spots based on their reviews: [PASTE REVIEWS]. Find the GAP I could own, in one sentence.

TIP One-star reviews are a gift — they tell you exactly what a competitor's customers wish existed instead.

04

The Offer Sketch

Something small enough to sell this week

Design the smallest offer I could sell in [NICHE] within a week: format, price, what's included, delivery, and a one-sentence promise. Then a BIGGER version I could build later if the first one sells.

TIP Small and shippable beats big and perfect — you're testing demand, not launching a company yet.

05

The 48-Hour Test

A landing page and 100 visitors

Plan a 48-hour test for [OFFER] in [NICHE]: the landing page text (headline, 3 bullets, button), where to get the first 100 visitors for free, what to measure, and the result that means GO, TWEAK or KILL.

TIP Decide your GO number before you launch the page — it's too easy to move the goalpost once results come in.

06

The Name & Angle

Stand out from the shelf in one line

Suggest 10 names and 5 positioning angles for my offer in [NICHE] for [AUDIENCE]. Avoid generic names, check that the angle is different from [COMPETITORS], and pick the BEST COMBINATION with the reason.

TIP Say the name out loud and picture it in a comment reply — if it's hard to say, it's hard to remember.

07

The Kill-or-Continue

Decide with numbers, not hope

Here are my test results for [OFFER]: [VISITORS] visitors, [SIGNUPS] signups, [SALES] sales, plus these comments: [PASTE]. Should I continue, change the offer, change the audience, or stop? Explain the decision like an HONEST INVESTOR.

TIP An honest investor kills a losing idea in a day — don't let sunk cost keep a dead niche on life support.
